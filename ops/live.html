The business exists so that a person can live. Not the reverse.

Almost every business I have seen is arranged the other way round. The person is the fuel. Their evenings are the runway, their health is the working capital, and the thing they built becomes the thing they answer to.

It arrives politely. A quiet week becomes a busy one. The work that pays gets done on Sunday because Sunday was free. Three nights under six hours is not a decision anyone makes; it is a subtraction nobody watched. Then a year has gone and the business is real, and the life it was supposed to fund is the thing on the invoice.

What I mean by living

Not leisure, and not balance as an aesthetic. Four ordinary things, in this order:

  1. Sleep is not negotiable.Three consecutive nights under six hours is an incident, not a work ethic. Everything downstream — judgement, temper, the quality of a decision at 4pm — is paid for out of it.
  2. The hours are fixed and the work fits inside them.A day has a shape. If the work does not fit the shape, the work is wrong, not the day. That is why the estate runs on roughly three hours a week of a human's attention, and why that number is allowed to be the constraint rather than the aspiration.
  3. Nothing important depends on me being reachable.A business that stops when the phone is off is a job with extra steps. The measure is not how much I do; it is what keeps happening while I am not looking.
  4. The money is a floor, not a score.Enough, steady, and honest about its costs. A number that grows while the person shrinks is not growth; it is a slow transfer.
A machine can send at 3am. It should not. That is not a limit on the machine — it is a limit on the person who would otherwise be awake to supervise it.

The same logic reaches everything the estate does. The three-touch sequence stops after three touches, because persistence is a virtue and a nuisance is a cost somebody else pays. A reply is handed to a person, because a reply is a person. The daily screen ends with one instruction: close the laptop and leave the room. That is not a wellness tip appended to a business plan; it is the test the plan has to pass.

The counter-test

Ask these in this order. The first one that fails is the thing to fix this week.

  • If I did nothing for seven days, what stops?
  • If it stopped, would anyone but me notice?
  • If the answer is nobody — is that the business failing, or am I?
  • What did I last do that had no return except that I wanted to?

The business exists so that a person can live. Not the reverse. Written in Harare, on a phone, on a Tuesday.
